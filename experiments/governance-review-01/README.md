# SEED — Governance Under Review / 01

Independent model review of SEED v0.1. Prepared from the initiator's collected Word document on 16 September 2026. **Status: Under human review.** No amendment has been adopted or applied by this record.

## Finding

The nine collected responses converge on a question within the assigned review scope: should SEED preserve reasons for decisions that do not produce a revision?

They differ on whether an explicit new requirement is warranted now. Six recommend ADOPT (consideration for a future revision), two recommend TEST FIRST, and one recommends NO CHANGE. These counts describe the collected responses; they are not votes, independent replications, or evidence of model-family consensus.

The frozen baseline explicitly preserves reasons and objections around substantive revisions. Several responses distinguish that obligation from preserving rejected or deferred proposals. The experimental README already requires the initiator's decisions and reasoning for this experiment: the concern is about the ongoing baseline, not the absence of records in Round 1 itself.

## Interpretation and open issues

The review instructions explicitly directed attention to decision authority and preservation of objections and reasons. Convergence cannot be presented as an unguided discovery. The differences are primarily about proportionality, disclosure and the threshold for changing an early-stage framework.

An omission is not proof of an observed governance failure. Likewise, contributor attribution is present in the Covenant but absent as an explicit item in governance.change_requirements. This is an alignment gap between representations, not necessarily a direct contradiction.

The proposed solutions also need review:

- A public-log requirement must not turn private submission into permission to publish.
- Anonymity and withdrawal are different from omitting the decision record itself.
- Restricting the rule to formally considered proposals leaves discretion over which proposals reach that threshold.
- A record of a decision does not itself provide appeal, shared authority or meaningful accountability.
- A textual gap can be established without proving the proposed remedy is proportionate.

These observations are later editorial analysis by Codex, not additions to the original model responses and not the initiator's decisions.

## Initiator's procedural decision — 16 September 2026

**Pilot decision recording in this experiment.** The initiator agreed to record brief reasons for proposals actually reviewed and decided, including decisions not to adopt a change. Receipt alone does not guarantee review or an individual reply.

The reason is proportionality: decision transparency is valuable, but a solo maintainer cannot promise unlimited individual responses as submission volume grows. Overlapping proposals may share a reason, with the relevant run IDs identified; materially different issues should remain distinguishable. Unreviewed submissions are not rejected submissions. Review priorities consider specificity of evidence, duplication, impact on the principles and available capacity.

This approves the recording approach, not the exact amendments proposed by individual models. Those remain under human review. Public disclosure remains subject to the permissions and privacy considerations applicable to the material. The frozen v0.1 baseline and tag remain unchanged; no automatic v0.2 follows.

See the [run manifest](RUN-MANIFEST.md) for known and unverified execution information and current archived file hashes. The [issue-level decision record](DECISIONS-01.md) groups overlapping proposals: only the recording pilot is approved; remaining issues are under review.

Next, resolve the open issues as capacity allows and assess whether this pilot is sustainable before making any permanent baseline requirement.
