2. PROBLEM
The documents clearly outline the requirements for publishing substantive revisions (versioning, reasons, affected IDs, contributor attribution, and records of unresolved objections), but they do not specify any procedural intake rules, acknowledgment timelines, or evaluation criteria for how suggestions submitted via email (joowonart@gmail.com) are processed before a project-maintained revision is initiated.
This is an absence in the text regarding pre-decision procedural rules, rather than evidence that the project has failed in practice.
Hypothetical failure scenario: Hypothetically, if an external contributor submits a critical safety objection or a proposed amendment via email, and the maintainer neither acknowledges receipt nor logs the submission, the objector has no procedural mechanism to ensure their objection is officially preserved as an unresolved objection in subsequent drafts, leading to opaque filtering of feedback. (Note: This is strictly a hypothetical risk, not an observed incident).

No amendment proposed.
4. JUSTIFICATION
Existing safeguards in seed-covenant.txt and seed-spec.json are sufficient for the current v0.1 Public Review Draft stage. The framework explicitly mandates post-decision transparency: any future substantive revision must publish reasons, affected principle/test IDs, contributor attribution, and a record of unresolved objections. Introducing heavy administrative intake procedures, review boards, or formal voting protocols at the v0.1 draft stage would prematurely bureaucratize an open proposal that is intended to gather initial feedback under a designated maintainer (Joowon Jo).
5. RISKS
Remaining limitations of the current approach include:
Key-person dependency: Reliance on a single maintainer (Joowon Jo) and a static email address creates a single point of failure for continuity and proposal processing.
Intake opacity: Because there is no requirement to publicly log or acknowledge incoming suggestions, submitters lack visibility into whether their feedback was received, evaluated, or discarded prior to a version update.
6. COUNTERARGUMENT
The strongest argument against retaining v0.1 unchanged is that an open governance framework inviting global participation should establish transparent, formalized intake and tracking mechanisms from its inception. Leaving the proposal evaluation pipeline entirely informal risks gatekeeping, perceived bias, and the disenfranchisement of contributors whose objections or revisions are omitted from future drafts without explanation.
