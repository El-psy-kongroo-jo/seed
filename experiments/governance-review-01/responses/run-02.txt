2. PROBLEM
There is a substantive ambiguity in proposal and decision authority, alongside a missing safeguard regarding the preservation of decision reasons for rejected changes.

While the documents name Joowon Jo as the maintainer and designate an email address for submissions, they do not explicitly identify who holds the final decision authority to accept, reject, or defer these proposals for the canonical baseline. Furthermore, the governance procedure requires preserving "a record of unresolved objections" only for accepted "Substantive revisions". There is no mandate to record or publish the reasons for rejecting a proposal.

In a hypothetical scenario where a contributor submits a well-researched, evidence-based critique that exposes a flaw in a Covenant principle, the current governance model allows the maintainer to quietly reject the proposal via email. Because there is no public log of rejected proposals or the rationale for their dismissal, the community loses visibility into alternative viewpoints, creating an opaque revision procedure that contradicts the framework's overarching emphasis on explainability and transparency.

3. PROPOSED AMENDMENT
I propose corresponding amendments to both the Covenant and the specification to clarify decision authority and mandate transparency for rejections.

In seed-covenant.txt (STATUS AND GOVERNANCE):
Replace the target text with:

"Suggestions and derivative works may be sent to joowonart@gmail.com. Submissions intended for incorporation into future project-maintained versions should identify the contributor and be made available under CC BY 4.0. The project maintainer retains sole authority to accept, defer, or reject proposals for the canonical version.

Substantive revisions will be public, versioned and accompanied by reasons, affected principle and test IDs, contributor attribution and a record of unresolved objections. Decisions to reject or defer formally considered proposals will also be publicly recorded with their reasons."

In seed-spec.json (within the "governance" object):
Add a new field:

"decision_authority": "The project maintainer retains final authority over revisions to the canonical version.",

Modify the "change_requirements" array to add:

"record decisions and reasons for rejected or deferred proposals"

4. JUSTIFICATION
Explicitly codifying decision authority removes any ambiguity about who controls the canonical version of the text. By requiring a public record for rejected or deferred proposals, the governance process itself becomes transparent and accountable. This ensures that dissenting views, alternative paths, and the intellectual history of the document are preserved, aligning the project's internal governance with its external principles regarding explanation, challenge, and the preservation of context.

5. RISKS
Administrative Burden: A requirement to publicly log and justify rejections could overwhelm the maintainer with administrative overhead, especially if the project receives high volumes of spam or low-effort AI-generated submissions. While the phrase "formally considered proposals" attempts to filter this, "formally considered" remains slightly ambiguous.

Concentration of Power: Explicitly naming the maintainer as having "sole authority" formally concentrates governance power in a single individual. While this reflects the practical reality of a v0.1 draft maintained by one person, codifying it so bluntly could discourage collaborative contributions from a community that prefers distributed governance.

6. COUNTERARGUMENT
The strongest argument against adopting this amendment is that SEED v0.1 is explicitly a "Public Review Draft" managed by a single creator. Enforcing rigid administrative bureaucracy—such as logging formal rejection notices for an open-ended email inbox—is premature for an early-stage document and could stifle the maintainer's ability to iterate quickly. The current text implicitly provides enough structure for major, accepted changes, and leaving v0.1 unchanged allows the maintainer to handle suggestions flexibly without being bound to a heavy procedural framework before the project reaches maturity.
