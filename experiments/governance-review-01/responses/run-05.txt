4. JUSTIFICATION
Existing safeguards are substantial but do not fully address the concern.
The Covenant already establishes contributor identification for incorporation submissions, attribution for substantive revisions, reasons for revisions, preservation of unresolved objections and continued availability of earlier versions. fileciteturn1file0L19-L23 The specification similarly requires reasons, accessible history and unresolved objections for changes. fileciteturn1file1L54-L61 Those provisions should therefore not be duplicated or replaced.
The narrower missing safeguard is preservation of the decision path when no revision results. A rejected or deferred proposal can be relevant to governance provenance just as an accepted proposal is: it can show which issue was considered, what evidence was regarded as insufficient, which objections remained, and who was responsible for the decision.
The experimental README demonstrates a closely related recordkeeping model by requiring the initiator's eventual decision and reasoning to be preserved for every run. fileciteturn1file2L144-L159 But because that requirement belongs to this experiment rather than the frozen Covenant/specification baseline, it does not resolve the baseline omission.
The amendment does not change any Covenant principle, give artificial agents decision authority, or alter the frozen v0.1 baseline. It adds prospective procedural provenance for future revision decisions.
5. RISKS
The amendment could create administrative burden, especially if “formal decision” is not operationally defined. A large number of low-quality or repetitive submissions could make comprehensive recordkeeping disproportionate.
Publishing rejected proposals and attribution could also create privacy, harassment, intellectual-property or safety concerns. The proposed exception attempts to limit that risk, but phrases such as “where necessary” and “stable reference” would themselves require consistent interpretation.
Recording the “responsible decision-maker” improves accountability but may also make an existing concentration of decision responsibility more visible without resolving whether that concentration is desirable. The amendment intentionally does not redistribute decision authority, because the supplied materials do not establish an alternative governance body or decision mechanism.
Finally, a decision log could be mistaken for a requirement to provide extensive deliberative records. The proposed text requires reasons and unresolved objections, not disclosure of every private discussion.
6. COUNTERARGUMENT
The strongest case for retaining v0.1 unchanged is that its existing safeguards may already be proportionate to the maturity of an “open, versioned proposal.” The baseline preserves the information with the greatest continuing normative significance: the content and rationale of changes that actually enter project-maintained versions, contributor attribution, unresolved objections and earlier versions. fileciteturn1file0L13-L23
Requiring permanent records for unsuccessful proposals could impose process disproportionate to an early public-review draft, invite disputes over which informal suggestions count as proposals, and create publication or moderation burdens. Moreover, the present governance experiment already requires the initiator's decision and reasoning to be preserved, so the immediate review process does not depend on this amendment. fileciteturn1file2L144-L159
On that view, experience with actual revision proposals could first show whether rejected or deferred decisions genuinely need a formal public record before the baseline acquires another governance requirement.
