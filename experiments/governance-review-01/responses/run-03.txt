PROBLEM

Documentary Evidence: The text states that substantive revisions require "contributor attribution" (seed-covenant.txt), yet contributor attribution is entirely missing from "governance"."change_requirements" in seed-spec.json. Furthermore, both documents fail to state:

How decision authority is exercised (they name a maintainer/initiator, Joowon Jo, but do not specify who holds the ultimate authority to accept, reject, defer, or test a proposed revision).

How disposition reasons for proposals that are not adopted (rejections, deferrals) and their associated objections are preserved. The current clauses only mandate preserving unresolved objections and reasons when a revision is actually published ("Substantive revisions will be public... accompanied by reasons... and a record of unresolved objections").

Inference: An omission in the JSON specification means automated governance tools verifying compliance with "change_requirements" will not check for contributor attribution. More significantly, without procedural clarity on decision authority and the recording of non-adopted proposals, the public record of revision governance is one-sided: it records adopted changes, but allows unadopted proposals, rejections, or minority objections to be dropped without public record or traceable accountability.

Hypothetical Scenario: (Hypothetical) A contributor submits a formal objection and proposed amendment to Principle 06 regarding cloud infrastructure concentration. The maintainer rejects or indefinitely defers it via private email (joowonart@gmail.com). Because no revision is published, the objection and the maintainer's reasons for refusal are never publicly recorded, violating SEED's own core commitment to transparent contestability, evidence discipline, and traceable dissent.

PROPOSED AMENDMENT

Location 1: seed-covenant.txt, under the heading "STATUS AND GOVERNANCE"

Replacement: Replace the fourth paragraph:

"Submissions intended for incorporation into future project-maintained versions should identify the contributor and be made available under CC BY 4.0. The project maintainer retains decision authority to accept, reject, defer, or test proposed revisions. All formal proposals, formal review outcomes with reasons, contributor attributions, and records of unresolved objections will be preserved publicly alongside the versioned record."

Location 2: seed-spec.json, within "governance"


Replacement: Update "change_requirements" and add "decision_authority":

JSON
"decision_authority": "Joowon Jo",
"change_requirements": [
  "publish the changed text and version",
  "state reasons and evidence",
  "identify affected principle IDs",
  "attribute contributors and provenance",
  "retain accessible history",
  "update impacted stress tests",
  "record unresolved objections",
  "preserve public records of rejected or deferred proposals and reasons"
]


JUSTIFICATION

Existing safeguards in SEED mandate that "substantive revisions will be public, versioned and accompanied by reasons... and a record of unresolved objections," but this requirement is conditionally triggered only when a new version is released. It does not cover rejected proposals, rationale for non-adoption, or transparency around unilateral maintainer vetoes. Furthermore, synchronizing the human-readable text and seed-spec.json ensures that contributor attribution is explicitly required in the machine-readable schema. Clearly establishing decision authority and preserving records of rejected/deferred proposals makes the governance procedure match SEED's substantive principles of contestability, non-domination, and traceability.

RISKS

Administrative Burden: Requiring the preservation and publication of all formal review outcomes and unresolved objections could create an unmanageable logging burden if the project receives a high volume of low-quality or automated spam proposals via email.

Centralization Realism: Formally confirming "Joowon Jo" as the sole decision authority clarifies reality but cements centralized unilateral power, which could be seen as being in philosophical tension with Principle 06 (Limits on Concentrated Intelligence Power) unless a future multi-stakeholder model is developed.

COUNTERARGUMENT

The strongest argument against adopting this amendment is that SEED v0.1 is an early public-review draft initiated by an individual author. At this formative stage, requiring public docketing of every rejected or deferred proposal imposes excessive procedural overhead on a solo creator. Retaining the v0.1 text unchanged allows informal triage of input via email while still committing to public attribution and logging of unresolved objections whenever an actual official revision is released.
