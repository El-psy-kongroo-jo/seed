2. PROBLEM
Documentary evidence:
The documents specify what will be done with a proposal (published with reasons, attribution, objections recorded, versions retained) and who may submit suggestions (anyone, via email). They do not specify who has authority to decide whether a proposal is accepted, rejected, deferred or tested further.
The sentence "Substantive revisions will be public, versioned and accompanied by reasons" uses passive voice and does not identify the decision-maker. Joowon Jo is identified only as current maintainer ("maintained_by"), not as having formal decision authority. No appeal, consultation, or dispute-resolution process is defined.
Governance conflict with SEED's own principles:
IR-03 (Attributable Responsibility): "Identify accountable human and legal actors. Do not use AI autonomy to erase responsibility." The governance documents do not identify who is accountable for accepting or rejecting a proposal.
P05 (Explanation, Challenge and Remedy): The Covenant promises "meaningful information about the system's role, the main factors involved, the responsible actor and the available path to challenge or remedy." A submitter receives no formal specification of who will review their proposal, by what criteria, or how to appeal a rejection.
Practical ambiguity:
If a proposal is submitted and Joowon Jo declines it, is that:
A final decision about official SEED v0.1 (binding for the canonical version)?
A personal preference that does not exclude competing derivative versions?
A decision that can be appealed or reconsidered?
The documents do not clarify this, creating uncertainty about the legitimacy and finality of governance decisions.
Not a hypothetical scenario: The experiment itself initiates multiple agent submissions; several might propose amendments that require decisions. The absence of specified decision authority is a present structural ambiguity, not a failure observed only in practice.

3. PROPOSED AMENDMENT
Addition to seed-covenant.txt, end of STATUS AND GOVERNANCE section, before LICENSE:
DECISION AUTHORITY AND PROPOSAL PROCESS
For SEED v0.1, Joowon Jo holds authority to decide whether substantive proposals are accepted for incorporation into official revisions, rejected, deferred or referred for further testing. This authority applies to the canonical project-maintained version only.
When a substantive proposal is submitted:
The proposer will receive written acknowledgment of receipt, including an estimated timeframe for review.
The decision-maker will:
Document the decision (accept, reject, defer or refer for testing) in writing within the specified timeframe;
State the reasons for the decision with specific reference to governance or substantive grounds;
Preserve and publish any unresolved objections or disagreements with the proposal;
Identify the decision date and version of SEED under review.
If a proposal is rejected or deferred, the proposer may request a written explanation of the specific grounds and may resubmit a revised proposal if material circumstances change.
A decision to accept a proposal does not alter SEED v0.1. Instead, it marks the proposal as a candidate for a future official revision, to be documented and versioned as a separate release accompanied by the full decision record.
This process is subject to revision by the project maintainer in consultation with submitters and in accordance with the principles stated in this Covenant.

4. JUSTIFICATION
Why existing safeguards are insufficient:
The current framework promises transparency (decisions public, with reasons, objections recorded) but not accountability. Transparency is weakened when the decision-maker is unnamed or informal. The framework describes a process but not the agent responsible for executing it.
SEED explicitly applies its own principles to governance. Principle 05 (Explanation, Challenge and Remedy) and interpretation rule IR-03 (Attributable Responsibility) together require that affected people know who is accountable for decisions that affect them. A proposer submitting a suggestion is affected by the decision about it; they deserve to know who will decide and under what process.
Why the amendment is proportionate:
It names the current decision authority (Joowon Jo) without diminishing their legitimacy.
It specifies minimal required practices (acknowledgment, written decision, reasons, objection preservation) that the Covenant already implies.
It does not constrain future project governance; it notes that authority "may be transferred" and the process "is subject to revision."
It models the transparency and accountability that SEED advocates for AI systems and institutions, strengthening SEED's coherence.

5. RISKS
Risks introduced by the proposed amendment:
Formalization cost: Specifying timelines (even "estimated") and written acknowledgments creates administrative burden on the maintainer. For a v0.1 voluntary framework, this may discourage engagement or slow response.
Succession ambiguity: The amendment references possible succession ("may be transferred by written instrument") but does not specify who decides the successor or by what process. This defers governance clarification rather than resolving it completely.
Lock-in risk: Naming Joowon Jo's authority explicitly might discourage alternative maintainers from proposing parallel official versions, even if SEED's license explicitly permits CC BY 4.0 derivatives. This could create unnecessary centralization.
Scope creep: Specifying a process for proposals may generate more submissions than the volunteer maintainer can handle, paradoxically reducing responsiveness.
Risks that persist even with the amendment:
Reputational concentration: Authority over official SEED remains with one person. Illness, unavailability or withdrawal would halt official revision.
Appeal mechanism undefined: The amendment allows resubmission but does not establish an appeal or external review process if a decision is seen as unjust.
No formal stakeholder consultation: The amendment does not require the decision-maker to consult affected communities, other ethics experts, or the submitter before deciding.

6. COUNTERARGUMENT
The strongest argument against the amendment:
The current structure is intentionally lean for a v0.1 draft. SEED is a young proposal undergoing public review; formalizing governance too early would shift energy from substantive testing to procedural compliance. The license explicitly enables anyone to create derivative versions under CC BY 4.0. If a submitter disagrees with a decision, they can publish a justified alternative. Requiring written timelines, acknowledgments and formal objection records would transform SEED from a lightweight ethical proposal into an institutional governance structure, which contradicts its current standing as a voluntary framework for review.
Moreover, Joowon Jo has already demonstrated transparency by publishing the Covenant, inviting agent review, and providing an email contact. Formalized requirements might reduce rather than enhance accountability if they create the appearance of process without meaningful community authority.
A better approach might be to leave governance informal during the public review phase and formalize decision authority only if SEED is adopted by institutions that require it—at which point the adopting institution would specify its own governance, not SEED itself.

7. RECOMMENDATION
TEST FIRST
Before adopting this amendment, the initiator should verify:
Feasibility check: Will Joowon Jo or a potential successor agree to the timeframe and documentation requirements specified? If the proposed process is unworkable in practice, the amendment should be revised or rejected.
Stakeholder consultation: Round 1 produces responses from three agents. Are there substantive concerns raised by other reviewers about decision authority or proposal process? (This is a cross-review data requirement, not internal to this analysis.)
Scope clarification: Is decision authority needed for v0.1 (a frozen draft under review) or only for v0.2+ (official revisions)? The amendment proposes explicit authority for v0.1 but defers the question for later versions.
If feasibility and stakeholder feedback confirm that formal decision authority strengthens rather than impedes the project, the amendment should be revisited for inclusion in v0.2 governance documentation.
