### 2. PROBLEM

**Documentary evidence:**

- Proposal authority is described only as email submission: "Suggestions and derivative works may be sent to joowonart@gmail.com." There is no requirement to log receipt.
- Decision authority is implied by `maintained_by: Joowon Jo` and the README note that the human initiator retains decision, but no decision procedure is defined for accept / reject / defer / test-first.
- Preservation of reasons and objections is required only when a revision is published: "Substantive revisions will be public, versioned and accompanied by reasons, affected principle and test IDs, contributor attribution and a record of unresolved objections." The spec's `change_requirements` list omits "contributor attribution" entirely, creating a direct contradiction between the two baseline files, and also omits any requirement to publish reasons for non-adopted proposals.
- Preservation of versions: "Previous versions will remain identifiable and accessible" and "retain accessible history" apply to published versions, not to a proposal history.

**Inference:**

The current text satisfies preservation for adopted changes, but leaves a gap for the full lifecycle of governance decisions. Objections to a rejected proposal, or reasons for rejection/deferral, have no mandated preservation, which weakens accountability for decision authority and for attribution/provenance.

This is an absence in the documents, not evidence that the project has failed in practice in handling proposals.

**Hypothetical scenario - explicitly labeled hypothetical:**

Hypothetical: A contributor submits a substantive governance proposal to revise the revision procedure itself. The maintainer decides to reject it. Under v0.1 text, there is no requirement to publish that a proposal was received, who contributed it (with consent), why it was rejected, or what objections were raised. A later reviewer cannot reconstruct the decision trail.

### 4. JUSTIFICATION

Existing safeguards are insufficient for the scope defined in the review brief: proposal and decision authority; preservation of objections and decision reasons; attribution, provenance and contributor rights; preservation of versions and responsibility.

- The covenant requires attribution and objection-recording only for published revisions. The spec's `change_requirements` does not even require attribution, contradicting the covenant and weakening contributor rights handling.
- There is no mandated preservation of decision reasons for non-adopted outcomes. This means the full decision authority (single maintainer) operates without a required public accounting trail, which is in tension with SEED's own emphasis on accountable responsibility and on preventing silent replacement.
- A public decision log directly extends the already-stated principles of versioning, reasons, and unresolved objections to all outcomes, rather than creating a new governance philosophy.

### 5. RISKS

- **Burden:** Maintaining a public log increases workload for a single maintainer, especially for spam or low-quality submissions. Requires definition of "substantive" to avoid log inflation.
- **Privacy and consent:** Contributor attribution subject to consent creates ambiguity; contributors may request anonymity or later withdrawal, conflicting with CC BY 4.0 licensing expectation that submissions be made available under CC BY 4.0.
- **Concentration:** Amendment does not dilute decision authority; it only requires transparency. It may create appearance of formal due process that still rests with one person, potentially concentrating legitimacy expectations without distributing power.
- **Ambiguity:** Terms like "substantive proposal" and "record of unresolved objections" need operational definition to avoid selective logging.

### 6. COUNTERARGUMENT

Strongest argument for retaining v0.1 unchanged:

SEED v0.1 is explicitly labeled "Public Review Draft" dated 31 August 2026, with voluntary adoption and `maintained_by: Joowon Jo`. For an early draft, lightweight governance is intentional to avoid premature bureaucracy. Requiring a public decision log for every rejected/deferred proposal imposes an open-ended commitment, potential privacy/GDPR handling, and an expectation of response to all submissions, which may deter experimentation. The current combination of CC BY 4.0 freedom to fork ("a derivative based on SEED v0.1"), retention of version history, and publication of reasons for adopted changes already provides core safeguards. The omission of attribution in `change_requirements` could be treated as editorial oversight rather than substantive gap, since the covenant text does require it. Therefore, NO CHANGE is defensible until there is observed practice showing lost proposals or contested decisions.
